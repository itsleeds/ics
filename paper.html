Open-Source Tools for Policy Delivery

Evidence from English Local Cycling and Walking Infrastructure Plans (LCWIPs) and Active Travel Fund Allocations

Abstract

Governments worldwide are committing billions in capital funding toward active travel infrastructure to meet net-zero, public health, and urban mobility targets. However, funding alone does not guarantee cost-effective or joined-up network delivery. This paper evaluates the multi-year implementation of the open-source Propensity to Cycle Tool (PCT) and its role in supporting England’s parallel statutory planning policy, the Local Cycling and Walking Infrastructure Plan (LCWIP) framework (2017–2026). Using a reproducible automated pipeline combining LLM-assisted document extraction across published LCWIP strategies with official Department for Transport (DfT) and Active Travel England (ATE) funding datasets, we analyse the depth of tool adoption and its alignment with £1.5B+ in capital and revenue allocations. We find that 91% of analysed transport authorities embed the PCT, with 95% using it substantively for route prioritisation, corridor design, and scenario validation. Furthermore, 93–95% of national funding flowed to authorities adopting the tool, with adopters receiving over double the average funding of non-adopters (£9.4m vs £4.5m). We conclude with a core policy recommendation: when governments fund active travel infrastructure, they should simultaneously commission and maintain open-source planning tools to build local authority capacity, ensure transparent scheme prioritisation, and maximise the return on public investment.

1 Introduction

The transition toward sustainable transport systems requires unprecedented investment in dedicated walking and cycling infrastructure. However, funding allocations frequently suffer from fragmented planning, ad-hoc project selection, and variable analytical capability across sub-national authorities.

This paper investigates how the provision of an open-source, evidence-based planning tool—the Propensity to Cycle Tool (PCT)—supported the implementation of national active travel policy in England over nearly a decade. We explore how an openly available digital tool became embedded in statutory local plans and evaluate the relationship between tool usage and capital funding allocation.

A core message of this work is that when national governments establish capital funds for active travel, they must concurrently invest in open-source planning tools and data infrastructure. Providing accessible tools levels the playing field across resource-constrained local authorities, enhances scheme quality, and maximises the cost-effectiveness of taxpayer investment.
